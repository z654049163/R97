[Slide 12] ①Two main business letter formats
[Slide 12] ●Full  block  format
[Slide 12] This format  is  very  modern
[Slide 12] Every  line  of  the  letter  begins  at  the  left margin(flush   left)
[Slide 12] A  space   of  one   line   between   paragraphs.
[Slide 12] Open   punctuation: no   punctuation   marks outside the  body  of the  letter,except for  a comma after the salutation and the complementary close respectively.
[Slide 13] Learning Aids
[Slide 13] ①Two main business letter formats
[Slide 13] ●Semi-block/ indented  format
[Slide 13] The date and the complimentary  close  near  the  right  margin.
[Slide 13] The  first  line  of  each paragraph is indented,with no space of one line between paragraphs.
[Slide 13] If  the  letterhead  isn't  preprinted  on  the  stationery,you  should   put  it directly above the date.
[Slide 14] Learning Aids
[Slide 14] ②Explanation  of  invitation  letters
[Slide 14] ●Letterhead
[Slide 14] It usually appears at  the  very  top  of  the  letter,either  in  the  left  corner,right  corner,or  at the  center.Wherever  you  put  it,be  sure  that  your  address  is  complete.
[Slide 14] e.g.:
[Slide 14] School of Law
[Slide 14] Shandong University
[Slide 14] Jinan,Shandong 250100
[Slide 14] P.R.China
[Slide 15] Learning Aids
[Slide 15] ●Date
[Slide 15] Put  down  the  full  date:day,month,and  year.It  comes   right  after  and just below your address.It is a common practice to put the date against the  left  margin  in the full  block  style.The date of the  letter should  be quite clear.The year should be given in full,the month should be written in words to avoid misunderstanding and confusion,and the day may be written in cardinal or  ordinal  numbers  and  may be put before(British style) or after (American  style) the  month.
[Slide 15] British Style:8 May,2006      8th March,2006
[Slide 15] American  Style:May  8,2006       March  8th,2004
[Slide 16] Learning Aids
[Slide 16] Inside   address
[Slide 16] Always on the left margin.
[Slide 16] Courtesy title (e.g .Mrs., Ms.,Miss,Mr.), Professional  title(e.g.Dr.,Professor)
[Slide 16] Business title (e.g.Manager, Director), Courtesy and  professional titles always appear  before the name while  business  titles  come  after  the  name.
[Slide 16] Never  use  business titles  in  the  salutation.
[Slide 16] Remember  to  make  the  inside  address  as complete as possible.
[Slide 17] Learning Aids
[Slide 17] Salutation
[Slide 17] This is a greeting to the recipient.
[Slide 17] e.g.Dear Ms.Ann Mellon (use Ms. for a woman recipient when her marital status is not known or when she prefers Ms.),or Mr.John Wood,or Dear Professor Williams.
[Slide 17] Salutation is also always on the left margin.

[Slide 18] Body of the letter

[Slide 19] Either on the left-hand side or right- hand side of the page,with the first letter capitalized,and followed by a comma.
[Slide 19] Should  reflect your relationship with the recipient.

[Slide 22] ●Enclosure(s)(if any)
[Slide 22] This can be used to remind the recipient of the additional material(s) put in the invitation letter, such as an agenda of a conference or a curriculum vitae in an application letter. Some prefer the abbreviation Encl.
[Slide 23] Learning Aids
[Slide 23] ③Tips  for  writing  an  invitation  letter 1/3
[Slide 23] Make  sure  invitation  letters  are  cordial  and  enthusiastic.
[Slide 23] Send  invitation  letters  out  with  ample  advance  notice.
[Slide 23] Give  the  name  of  the   conference,the  time  and  the  venue  at  the beginning.
[Slide 23] Do  not  use  abbreviations  in  formal  invitations  except  for  name titles such as Mr., Mrs.,Ms.and   Dr.
[Slide 24] Learning Aids
[Slide 24] ③Tips  for  writing  an  invitation  letter 2/3
[Slide 24] (Optionally)Include  any  accommodations  that  will   be   made,such  as  lodging,  meals  and  transportation.
[Slide 24] Give the  name of the contact  person along with  phone  numbers and address(including email).
[Slide 25] Learning Aids
[Slide 25] ③Tips  for  writing  an  invitation  letter 3/3
[Slide 25] If you invite someone to speak at a conference/seminar,you should also  mention the type of speech,the topic or the scope and how long the speech will be.
[Slide 25] If  a  reply  is  required,place  R.S.V.P.(Please  reply)at  the  end  of  the invitation,often at the  left  corner.
[Slide 25] Check your letter for typos or any other mistakes in language,such as grammar,spelling,structure,etc.
[Slide 25] Keep the letter concise,factual,and to the point with a polite tone.And use language and terminology familiar to the intended recipients.
[Slide 26] A letter of acceptance is to inform the conference host/organizer of your acceptance of the invitation and attendance at the conference.
[Slide 26] A letter of refusal is written if you cannot accept the invitation for one reason or another. You should politely and promptly write to the organizer to decline the invitation. By doing so, you allow them time for arranging backup plans.

[Slide 30] Tips for Letter Writing
[Slide 30] Good letter writing includes proper grammar, punctuation, capitalization, format, structure and addressing persons of title.
[Slide 30] Be consistent in format.
[Slide 30] Be brief, succinct, and unambiguous in content.
[Slide 30] Be courteous and polite in tone.
[Slide 31] Learning Aids
[Slide 31] ①Tips for writing a letter of acceptance
[Slide 31] Your thanks are  usually expressed at the very  beginning of the  letter, but sometimes they can also be placed at the end of the first paragraph.
[Slide 31] An acceptance should be brief.It is considered to be good to simply state your acceptance and briefly mention the main contents of the invitation.
[Slide 32] Learning Aids
[Slide 32] If  necessary,inquire  about   particular   information  such  as  accommodations  during  the  conference,equipment  for  your  speech  or presentation at the conference/seminar,etc.
[Slide 32] Once  you   agree  to   accept  the   invitation,try  to   avoid   ungracious  amplifications.For example,"I am very busy,but I can manage it or I will come if the situation permits."
[Slide 33] Learning Aids
[Slide 33] ②Tips for writing a letter of refusal
[Slide 33] Make a courteous,sincere  remark,and  present convincing  reason(s)for declining the invitation.
[Slide 33] Suggest  any  alternative  courses  of  action  or  other  resources,if appropriate.
[Slide 33] Express good wishes for the next cooperation in the future.
[Slide 34] Most organizations issue conference notices, invitations, calls for papers or notification of acceptance, etc. via email.
[Slide 34] Format for Writing an Email
[Slide 34] From: the name or email address of the addresser or the combination
[Slide 34] To: (the name and/or) the email address of the addressee
[Slide 34] Subject: the focus/topic of the email
[Slide 34] Body: purpose in writing the email, details, etc
[Slide 34] Conclusion: telling what is next or possibly explaining what a follow-up is required

[Slide 37] Learning Aids
[Slide 37] Tips for writing an effective email
[Slide 37] Start with thank-you remarks or your reason(s)for writing the email.
[Slide 37] Create  a  good  subject  line.The  subject  line  states  the  focus  of  your topic in your email.
[Slide 37] Identify  the  purpose  of  your  message.
[Slide 37] Place your most important information in the first paragraph,and even get the well-thought details packed into the first sentence or two.
[Slide 37] Keep your message clear and concise.
[Slide 37] Be careful when sending attachments.

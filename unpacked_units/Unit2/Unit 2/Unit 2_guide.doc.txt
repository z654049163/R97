International Letter ExchangesLearning Objectives Background Information Prior to the opening of a conference, a lot of work needs to be done: forming various committees, making contact with experts and scholars, booking venues, etc. The preparation involves enormous letter exchange or correspondences. Learning to write letters according to English conventions is an essential skill for graduate students. In this unit, students will get to know the most common letter formats: the full block format and semi-block format. They will learn how to write an invitation letter, accept an invitation or decline an invitation. As the Internet is spreading out all over the world with increasing accessibility, emails have largely taken place of traditional mails because of its ease and speed. In Task 3, students will also learn to write effective emails for academic purposes.  Illustration Introduction The teacher needs to bring the students attention to the format of letters and remind students that the English letters are different from Chinese letters in many aspects. Instruction should focus on the body of the letter: how to accept or refuse an invitation politely and conventionally (see PowerPoint). Encourage students to explore and generalize the rules for writing letters of acceptance or refusal. Acceptance and refusal letters are supposed to be short and succinct. Refrain from talking about anything irrelevant. Learning Aids Tips are available for teachers and students and would be better used with specific examples at the teacher’s discretion. Thus, it is important to expose students to a variety of sentence patterns and expressions to help them learn to vary their sentence patterns. The teacher needs to draw their attention to Useful Expressions and Sentence Patterns and encourage students to add sentence variety in writing practice. This task provides a perfect opportunity for peer writing or interactive writing. Students can work in pairs: one writing an invitation letter to invite his/her partner to a conference; the other writing to accept or decline the invitation. When the writing is finished, the teacher can have students read their work to each other and peer-review their letters. Illustration Introduction Writing effective emails is considered an essential skill in this electronic age. Students compose and check emails every day but most of them do that in Chinese. They are not aware of the difference between writing an English email and a Chinese one. Emails are practically letters in electronic form. In other words, emails are letters, so they should follow letter writing conventions including the format and the content. Sample Demonstration Most students have the background knowledge of composing an email. For instance, typing in the recipient’s email address and filling a subject in the subject line. Tell students that if they see Re: appear in the subject line (which usually automatically shows up when you click reply), it means “concerning” in Latin, not “reply.” When looking at the samples, students need to pay attention to both the format and the content. Learning Aids This section provides useful tips for writing emails. The teacher can have students read this section by themselves and use some questions to guide their reading and check if they understand these tips. Pedagogical Suggestions Choose one sample for demonstration to explain the format. Have students email each other to practice writing.  Students can practice writing with Exercises 2.References: http://jerz.setonhill.edu/writing/etext/e-mail.htmhttp://www.riches.com.au/articles/writing-email-tips.htmAdditional Samples   Ref:DM-113310 May 2004To:	International OrganizationsContact:WSIS Executive SecretariatTel:+41 22 730 6316Fax:+41 22 730 6393E-Mail:wsis@itu.int Subject:Preparatory Meeting (Organizational PrepCom), World Summit on the Information Society, Tunis Phase, Hammamet, Tunisia, 24-26 June 2004Dear Sir/Madam,It is my pleasure to invite you to attend the Preparatory Meeting of the Tunis Phase of the World Summit on the Information Society (WSIS) to be held in Hammamet, Tunisia, from 24 to 26 June 2004.In December 2003, the Geneva phase of the Summit successfully created a framework for an inclusive Information Society and the shared values needed to support it.

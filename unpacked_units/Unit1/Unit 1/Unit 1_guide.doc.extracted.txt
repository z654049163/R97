Preparation for International Conference
Learning Objectives 
Illustration 
Introduction 
A brief introduction of preparing conferences is provided to help students build background knowledge for further learning. The teacher can display some pictures of real conferences and explain how a conference is initiated and organized and the different functions of various committees. The focus should be on illustrating the purpose and format of call for papers. Refer to Learning Aids for some introductory information about conference preparation.
Sample Demonstration 
Samples from conferences of different disciplines are provided for students to get familiar with the format and content of call for papers. Students are guided to read and understand the text. Students may find that these samples are slightly different in terms of presentation (e.g., subheadings vs. no subheadings) and sentence patterns (e.g., passive voice vs. active voice). The teacher can explicitly explain to students that the purpose of writing determines the form. The forms/presentations may vary; however, no matter what form or sentence pattern is adopted, the call for papers should be clear about the conference theme/goals, issues/topics to be addressed, requirements for papers, and paper submission. Additional samples are available in this guide on Page 4.

This section provides basic information on preparation prior to a conference. The teacher can use it in the introduction part of the lesson.

Introduction 
A brief introduction of conference notice is provided to help students build background knowledge for further learning. The teacher should point out a conference notice sometimes overlaps with the call for papers in content, such as the theme, goals, background, etc; and highlight the difference: the conference notice often includes information about application, registration, accommodations, receptions, etc. 
Sample Demonstration 
Samples are provided for students to get familiar with the format and content of conference notice. Use Check Your Understanding to check if students have comprehended each sample. Additional samples are available in this guide. 
Learning Aids 
Remind students that writing a conference notice in English involves rearranging the order of information, adjusting the sentences or paragraphs, or leaving out some details in order to follow the English conventions. Again, the purpose determines the form of writing. Students should be aware of the characteristics of each genre of writing. 
Useful Expressions and Sentence Patterns  
Students should be familiar with these expressions and patterns and learn to use them when creating a conference notice. The teacher can select to explain some of the sentences that are not covered in the samples. Encourage students to find key verbs and highlight them. For sentence patterns, help students find the common threads of these sentences, i.e., passive voice, and learn how to use them in writing. 
Pedagogical Suggestions 
Choose one sample and guide the students through reading to explain some language points and the format. Divide students into groups to look at the remaining samples. 
Technology Integration. Download conference web pages and show the students a conference website and conference notice. Oftentimes, the conference notice appears as the home page on a conference website and in some occasions it is combined with the call for papers. Explain these tips to students and show them some samples. 
Find a conference website and download some information about the conference (preferably in the students
 area of study). Ask students to work in groups to develop a notice for this particular conference. 
Illustration 
Introduction 
The teacher should differentiate between agenda and program. An agenda is a list of meeting activities in the order in which they are to be taken up, beginning with the call to order and ending with adjournment. It usually includes one or more specific items of business to be considered. It may, but is not required to, include specific time for one or more activities. A conference often consists of multiple sessions and lasts a couple of days. A program acts as a guide for the conference participants, listing a series of activities to be done at a definite time and place. It helps participants fulfill their activities in accordance with the plan. In summary, an agenda is for a particular meeting while a program is usually adopted for a multiple-session conference to provide detailed information for each session or activity.

Help students find out the difference between an agenda and a conference program by reading and discussing these samples.

Design a capstone project, A Mock International Conference. Students are expected to prepare for, organize, and hold an international conference in their area of study. For instance, chemistry majors are going to host the 27th Chinese Chemistry Society Congress. They need to develop a call for papers, a notice, and a program for the conference. In the subsequent units (Unit 4 and 5), they will write speeches and make presentations to simulate a conference scenario. In this unit, the teacher should set up the background for students, so they know what they are expected to do and become more motivated.

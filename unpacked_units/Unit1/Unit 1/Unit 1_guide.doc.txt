The teacher can display some pictures of real conferences and explain how a conference is initiated and organized and the different functions of various committees. The focus should be on illustrating the purpose and format of call for papers. Refer to Learning Aids for some introductory information about conference preparation.Sample Demonstration Samples from conferences of different disciplines are provided for students to get familiar with the format and content of call for papers. Students are guided to read and understand the text. Students may find that these samples are slightly different in terms of presentation (e.g., subheadings vs. no subheadings) and sentence patterns (e.g., passive voice vs. active voice). The teacher can explicitly explain to students that the purpose of writing determines the form. The forms/presentations may vary; however, no matter what form or sentence pattern is adopted, the call for papers should be clear about the conference theme/goals, issues/topics to be addressed, requirements for papers, and paper submission. An agenda is a list of meeting activities in the order in which they are to be taken up, beginning with the call to order and ending with adjournment. It usually includes one or more specific items of business to be considered. It may, but is not required to, include specific time for one or more activities. A conference often consists of multiple sessions and lasts a couple of days. A program acts as a guide for the conference participants, listing a series of activities to be done at a definite time and place. It helps participants fulfill their activities in accordance with the plan. In summary, an agenda is for a particular meeting while a program is usually adopted for a multiple-session conference to provide detailed information for each session or activity. Sample Demonstration Help students find out the difference between an agenda and a conference program by reading and discussing these samples. Learning Aids The teacher can use a word web to illustrate the relationships between the superordinate (上义词) and hyponyms (下义词).Useful Expressions and Sentence Patterns  The teacher can go over these terms with the students and solicit the corresponding Chinese translation to help them understand these terms. Students are expected to prepare for, organize, and hold an international conference in their area of study. For instance, chemistry majors are going to host the 27th Chinese Chemistry Society Congress. They need to develop a call for papers, a notice, and a program for the conference. In the subsequent units (Unit 4 and 5), they will write speeches and make presentations to simulate a conference scenario. In this unit, the teacher should set up the background for students, so they know what they are expected to do and become more motivated.  Additional Samples 
